# ADR-0083 — The admin operations surface: ledger, appeals, strikes, escalation, webhooks

- Status: Proposed 2026-10-10; built in the same change behind migrations 0271 to 0276 (not yet applied). The webhook delivery log is behind `WEBHOOK_DELIVERY_LOG_ENABLED` ("false" in production, "true" on staging); everything else takes effect when the migrations are applied and the Worker is deployed. Owner sign-off is not yet recorded.
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Related: [ADR-0019](0019-dispute-webhooks-freeze.md) (Freeze), [ADR-0022](0022-manual-test-credit-grant.md) (manual grants need a reason naming the human), [ADR-0058](0058-byteplus-modelark-provider.md) decision 7 (warnings and takedowns), [ADR-0078](0078-admin-checks-in-code.md) (admin gates in code). Follows the 2026-10-10 research run on what an admin page for this product should see and do; the report is kept out of this repository.

## Problem

The admin page showed last-day metrics, a violations queue, a user lookup and the Cinema reviews, and could freeze or unfreeze an account. Five things an operator needs were missing, and three of them are not optional for a UK service with Cinema switched on:

1. **No ledger view.** A support ticket about a balance could only be answered by reading `ledger_entries` through the service role, and a goodwill grant was an SQL call recorded in an ADR by hand (ADR-0022).
2. **No appeal.** Ofcom's Illegal Content Codes of Practice (consolidated 9 September 2026, in force 30 September 2026) apply ICU D1, D2 and D10 to every user-to-user service regardless of size: a user must be able to contest a takedown, a warning or a suspension; a successful appeal must reverse the action; a pattern of wrong decisions must change the guidance. Cinema publishing and viewing are on in production, so the user-to-user test is met.
3. **One strike tier.** Three takedowns froze the account; nothing happened in between, and a takedown later found wrong still counted.
4. **No CSAM path.** The Thorn / All Tech Is Human *Safety by Design for Generative AI* standard (signed by Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Stability) asks a deployer to detect, to route to a moderation flow, to document the report-preserve-destroy procedure, to give users a real-time report path, and to enforce at user and model level. A takedown deleted the evidence the report needs.
5. **No view of refused webhooks.** A delivery refused at the door left a console line.

## Decision

Everything below is admin-only in the database (`users.is_admin`, raised as 42501 `not_admin`), behind the four gates of ADR-0078 in the route (`lib/adminGate.js`), and written to the append-only `account_actions` log with the admin's email as actor and a reason. Nothing is edited or deleted; corrections are new rows.

1. **Ledger drilldown and reasoned grant (0271).** `admin_user_ledger` returns one account's balance buckets, every ledger row with its reason and the action log. `admin_credit_grant` appends a Pack grant through `ledger_grant` with reason `grant:admin <email>` and the form's Idempotency-Key, and records `credit_grant` on `account_actions` with the operator's note, the ledger row and the amount. There is no negative path: Free and Subscription Credits have their own lifecycle and a Pack clawback is a separate decision. The note is for administrators; the user's statement files the row as an adjustment.
2. **Appeals (0272, 0273).** `content_appeals` holds one appeal per `account_actions` row for a warning, a takedown or a `content:` Freeze: the user's statement, and one decision with a reason. A user submits from `/app/account/appeals`; an admin decides at `/app/admin/appeals`. The decision is written to `account_actions` as `appeal_upheld` or `appeal_rejected`. Upholding a takedown takes it out of the strike count, recomputes the hold from the strikes that stand, and lifts a `content:` Freeze through `unfreeze_account` when fewer than three remain. Files already deleted are not restored. The user is emailed the outcome without the reason. The CSAM tier is not appealable.
3. **Strike ladder and generation hold (0272, 0274).** A takedown that stands is a strike. Strike 1 sets `users.hold_until` three days out, strike 2 seven days, strike 3 Freezes as before. A hold is never shortened by a later strike. The two admission functions (`private.ledger_debit_for_admission`, `private.submit_free_job_for_admission`) refuse `ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD` while the hold is in the future, so every paid and free generation path obeys it; buying credits, the Library and the account page do not. The model is Google Ads' strike ladder; the windowing is ours (no decay, standing strikes only).
4. **CSAM escalation and evidence hold (0275).** `record_csam_escalation` deletes the job's `assets` rows so nothing is served, moves the R2 keys to `evidence_holds` instead of the reap queue, removes them from the queue if already there, and Freezes the account with reason `content:csam_escalation`. No email is sent for this tier. `release_evidence_hold` writes `evidence_released` and queues the keys when the procedure allows. The procedure is `docs/agents/csam-escalation.md`: IWF or NCMEC within 24 hours, preserve 90 days or as long as law enforcement asks, record identifiers only.
5. **User reports (0275).** `content_reports`: a signed-in user reports a generation of their own (Report on the Library card), a Cinema title, or anything else, with a category; one open report per subject, at most twenty open per person. `/app/admin/reports` lists them CSAM first and closes each with a note.
6. **Webhook delivery log (0276).** `withDeliveryLog` wraps every inbound webhook route. After the handler answered, one row goes to `webhook_deliveries`: source, outcome class, HTTP status, typed error code. No payload, no headers. The answer the provider sees is never changed by the log, and a logging failure is swallowed. `/app/admin/webhooks` shows per-source counts by outcome, the refusals with the fix for each class, and `webhook_events` rows accepted but never processed. Rows older than thirty days are pruned opportunistically.

## Consequences

- Six migrations, all idempotent, none touching the money invariants: `reconcile_balances`, `reconcile_free_credits` and `reconcile_subscription_credits` return zero rows after a grant, a hold, an appeal and an escalation in the local smoke tests.
- `account_actions.action` gains `credit_grant`, `appeal_upheld`, `appeal_rejected`, `csam_escalation`, `evidence_released`; the table gains `ledger_entry_id`, `credits_delta`, `hold_until`. `users` gains `hold_until`.
- `record_content_violation` keeps its 0253 signature and answer shape, plus `hold_until`; `admin_lookup_user` and `list_content_violations` keep theirs with more fields. The violations page shows standing strikes, reversed ones, open appeals and the hold.
- `ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD` is a new typed refusal (403) from the generation, chat and fal dispatch paths, with its own copy in the create page and the chat.
- Every notice email now points at the appeal page; a takedown says until when generating is paused.
- Not built, on purpose: automatic scanning of prompts, uploads or outputs (a provider or hash-list decision with its own cost and agreement); a Pack clawback; moderator SLA dashboards and the other Ofcom measures that apply only above 7 million UK monthly users or to a multi-risk service; a bespoke creator payout view (Cinema payouts do not exist yet, ADR-0057).
- Open for the owner: whether the illegal-harms risk assessment makes this service multi-risk (which pulls ICU C3 to C8 in) or at medium or high risk of any single harm (which pulls D4 and D6, indicative complaint timeframes, in); whether a second administrator and two-person approval above a grant threshold are wanted before any grant is made in production.

## Amendment 1 (2026-10-11): clawback, image scanning, hook

An administrator can take back Pack credits using a negative amount on the existing ledger endpoint. The guarded clawback RPC requires fresh AAL2 within 300 seconds, serializes the globally unique key with grants, binds replay to user, amount, reason, actor and action, and appends a ledger entry plus an account action. Its original `balance_after` is stored for exact replay. Grant replay still reads the current balance; fixing that remains separate work. The Pack cap is `balance - COALESCE(free_balance, 0) - subscription_balance`: the entire stored Subscription bucket counts, including expired credits awaiting the hourly sweep, because `credit_balances_buckets_within_balance` requires both buckets to fit inside the total. The administrator UI uses the same formula. One administrator makes these decisions; there is no second approver.

Every newly stored `public.assets` image is enqueued by an insert trigger, including fal, kie, openrouter, grsai and clip edits. The trigger does not wait for a provider and catches enqueue errors so it cannot break `job_stored`. Enqueue runs while the scanner is off; that backlog drains oldest first after enabling. A non-partial asset uniqueness index prevents duplicate scans. Five-row leases last 120 seconds, carry fresh claim tokens, and permit five attempts; the claim after the fifth expired lease marks the scan unavailable. Stale tokens cannot decide a scan. The 45-second drain budget only stops additional claims; a claimed chunk finishes, so a run can take about 90 seconds. OpenAI calls have a 10-second timeout. Input errors 400/413/415 become unavailable immediately; transport failures and other failures wait for lease expiry.

The classifier is OpenAI `omni-moderation-latest`, using a 15-minute signed image URL. Hold thresholds are sexual ≥ 0.80, graphic violence ≥ 0.80, or a flagged self-harm, self-harm/intent or self-harm/instructions category. Other image-capable violence scores contribute to report ranking. `sexual/minors` is text-only and scores zero for image input, so this cannot automatically identify or escalate CSAM. A human reviews the report and decides whether to use the existing CSAM escalation. The scanner sends no email. Enabling the flag accepts this image data flow; OpenAI API data is not used for training by default.

A held asset receives no new ordinary serving link, appears as **Under review** in Library, and cannot be newly attached to a social post. The administrator can use **View held image** for a five-minute link; every inspection appends `held_asset_viewed`. Releasing a hold appends `hold_released`, clears the hold and resolves open scanner reports. Takedown deletes the asset but keeps the scan with a null asset ID; releasing after deletion cannot restore the file. As an owner-approved departure from the frozen plan, `content_scans.job_id` uses `ON DELETE CASCADE` so scans do not pin jobs or block account deletion.

`SOCIAL_HOLD_CHECKS_ENABLED` is false in production and true in staging. Apply migration 0289 first, then set both `SOCIAL_HOLD_CHECKS_ENABLED` and `CONTENT_SCAN_ENABLED` to `"true"`; an enabled sweep probes authorization availability before claiming any targets, and the drain logs an error while scanning is on and hold checks are off. Production publishing is unauthorised by the hold check until that flip: with the gate off, publishing continues without calling the authorization RPC. Keep both production flags off until the migration is applied. If the probe returns HTTP 404 / `PGRST202`, the sweep logs the missing function once and claims nothing, consuming no attempts. Per-dispatch missing-function deferral remains a second defence: if the function disappears after the probe, provider and completion calls are skipped, but an already-consumed claim attempt cannot be reversed. The apply-before-enable order remains mandatory.

Social claims exclude held media before claiming new dispatches, but still claim submitted targets for status checks. Dispatch authorization locks the target and source asset, checks the UUID claim key, and restores the pre-claim status, attempts and provider state on deferral. Both `MEDIA_HELD` and `CLAIM_LOST` skip without completing or consuming an attempt. Checks precede new media signing and each public write: Threads publication after container processing, YouTube upload chunks, and the final publish calls in Instagram, LinkedIn, X, Facebook, Pinterest, Bluesky and GMB. TikTok status, YouTube processing and upload offset probes, Threads status/permalink lookup, and stored-result reconciliation do not publish. A hold and authorization serialize on the asset lock; a hold committed after authorization returns leaves the residual window of the provider call's duration.

Scanning is post-storage. A new image can be served until a decision, normally under two minutes with the one-minute cron, but longer under backlog, retries or an OpenAI outage. Existing URLs are not revoked: the ordinary route signs for 15 minutes and the sweep uses `MEDIA_URL_TTL_SECONDS` (currently 900 seconds); previously issued social proxy tokens retain their own expiry. Unavailable scans never hold the image, so outages fail open. The reports page always displays the unavailable count from the last 24 hours, even with zero reports or a different report filter.

`CONTENT_SCAN_ENABLED` defaults to false in production and true in staging; `OPENAI_API_KEY` is optional while off. Both environments schedule one-minute and five-minute ticks. Existing recovery tasks remain on the five-minute schedule; scanning runs on both ticks, and leases make overlap safe. There is no inline webhook drain. An enabled drain arms database monitoring even if the key is missing; a missing key skips all claims and provider calls. This resolves the work order’s conflicting missing-key no-op and every-enabled-run arming requirements in favor of observability. While armed, the snapshot reports overdue after 20 minutes without a successful heartbeat (or after arming if none exists); failed drains with a heartbeat newer than arming enter the unhealthy-task path immediately, while absent or older heartbeats get the same 20-minute arming grace, backlog for queued or expired leases older than 30 minutes, and exhaustion above 20 unavailable decisions in 24 hours. Overdue has no expiry. Re-arming starts a fresh grace period. To turn scanning off, disable the flag, then use **Scanner: disarm monitoring** with a reason; that appends `scanner_disarmed`. Disarmed monitoring ignores old heartbeats entirely.

Cinema uploads, device image uploads (`social_uploads`), project uploads (`project_assets`), source upload reservations, generated video and text prompts remain outside this scanner. These need distinct publication holds and inspection/release paths. Pre-storage hash matching (IWF/PhotoDNA) remains roadmap D1. The hook now resolves the target file's git checkout root, preserving the existing database migration money-write exemption inside worktrees.

Known limits at merge, from the final independent inspection. Fix the first before `CONTENT_SCAN_ENABLED` is turned on in production.

- A failure that belongs to the whole provider (revoked or invalid key, quota, 429, 5xx) spends scan attempts like a per-image failure. A provider outage of about ten minutes can send the queued backlog to `unavailable`, which fails open and is never held. The exhaustion alert (more than 20 in 24 hours) and the failed heartbeat are the only signals. The fix is to give the attempt back on provider-wide failures.
- A social target claimed before the snapshot columns exist has no stored pre-claim status. If it is then deferred, it is restored as pending and one attempt is subtracted that the claim never added. The window is closed by applying the migration before either flag is turned on.
- The Threads two-hour `poll_timeout` now applies only to containers that are not yet finished, so a held target released late can still publish. This changes a shared adapter on purpose.
- Opening a held asset writes one `held_asset_viewed` row per request, so a retry or prefetch adds a duplicate row.
