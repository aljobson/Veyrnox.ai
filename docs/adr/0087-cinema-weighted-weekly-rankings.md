# ADR-0087 — Cinema weighted weekly rankings

- Status: implemented behind disabled rollout flags; live acceptance pending
- Date: 2026-10-11
- Owner decision: build the full weighted model from `docs/social-cinema/05-voting-ranking.md`, with moderator approval before publication and no cash prizes
- Migrations: 0281 audience signals and 0282 weighted rankings. Staging already has the independently prepared 0280 editor migration; no applied migration was renamed.

## Behavior

An entitled, authenticated playback can mint a private 15-minute watch session after its Stream token has been signed. Playback remains available if optional audience telemetry fails. The client reports conservative full-second timeline ranges only while playback is visible and running. The database bounds reports by one elapsed-time budget across all tabs and titles on an account, ties each session to the current upload, Stream UID and duration, and unions repeated coverage. A play-start billing minute is never a viewing signal. Stable keys bind the exact submitted payload on replay.

Successful copy and native share actions can record a first-party signal. Cancelling or failing a share records nothing. A fulfilled share action does not prove external delivery. Approved commenters without open reports contribute engagement. These signals use accounts, not verified unique people; client playback reports are not provider-certified delivery evidence. This does not implement device fingerprinting, cross-account fraud detection or third-party analytics.

The versioned `weighted-v1` model implements all five roadmap weights: qualified votes 40%, completion 25%, qualified audience 15%, sharing 10%, and engagement 10%. Count components use log scaling against the eligible week's maximum. Completion is a completion rate with four additional non-completing observations to temper a small sample. A title needs five qualified viewers; a viewer needs 30 seconds of unioned coverage or the entire duration of shorter media, and a completion needs 90%. These normalization and sample choices are proposed implementation defaults, requiring launch review before activation; the weights themselves are the existing roadmap model. The public explanation omits anti-abuse thresholds.

Episode signals can roll into a Series competition. Series completion considers qualified watched episodes, not every unwatched episode in its catalogue. Eligible media is at most ten minutes under the current upload limits. Existing qualified ballots keep their existing qualification rules and do not gain an invented retroactive watch prerequisite. Votes from frozen, suspended, deleted-Auth or creator-self accounts are excluded, as are invalid audience accounts. Story decision polls are independent of weekly ranking scores. Exact score ties share a rank; UUID order is only a stable presentation order.

Closed competitions of at most eight days can be prepared after their end time and held-ballot review. Calculation uses one MVCC query over actual eligible events; supplied counts or scores are rejected. The provisional snapshot and its publication/rejection decision are immutable. A different current Cinema administrator with fresh MFA and a verified person Access login must review publication. Neither reviewer can own a ranked title. Publication checks for unresolved held ballots and recalculates to reject stale scores. A rejected snapshot permits preparing again. There is no automatic winner publication, cash award, credit write or creator payout.

Public `/api/cinema/winners` exposes only published snapshots and current public title links, ranks and scores. It returns no actor identifiers or raw audience signals. Withdrawal/private visibility is rechecked on every read, including historical snapshots. Top three ranks may contain more than three titles when scores tie. Moderator `/app/admin/cinema/rankings` shows the component counts and audited decision form. Missing identity, stale MFA and service Access tokens fail closed.

## Controls and deployment

`CINEMA_AUDIENCE_ENABLED` and `CINEMA_RANKINGS_ENABLED` are false in staging and production source. Rankings also require Cinema profiles, viewing and voting; audience capture has a separate switch. New tables force RLS and revoke every direct role grant. RPCs have empty search paths and only the service role can execute exposed functions. The score calculator is internal. Row and standard statement-level TRUNCATE guards protect immutable signals/snapshots/decisions. Leading FK indexes and explicit request-size/rate limits are present. No token, IP address or device fingerprint is stored by this feature; private Stream identifiers remain server-only.

Production DDL must use the protected main workflow under ADR-0023. Preserve runtime variables and secrets during application deployment. Neither merging nor applying migrations enables these flags. Real Stream watch/seek/pause/renewal and cross-tab acceptance, actual qualified accounts, two-person moderator/Access/MFA acceptance, concurrency/load review and the relevant clean observation window remain required. Retention/deletion requirements for private event history need owner review; no unreviewed deletion path or indefinite-retention promise is introduced. Live historical rankings can outlast eligibility changes in their private immutable snapshot while public visibility still filters withdrawn titles.

This slice supplies the full weekly scoring engine and title/episode/series competition UI. Dedicated Rising Creator analytics and automated competition scheduling remain roadmap work.

## Verification

Local PostgreSQL 17 rebuilds the complete schema. The new actual SQL regression checks all five score components and manual score arithmetic, repeated coverage, account wall-clock bounds, foreign sessions, strict replay, held ballots discovered after preparation, stale comment eligibility, independent fresh-MFA publication, live withdrawal privacy, unchanged ledger, and RLS/ACL boundaries. Existing CI database scripts run against that fresh replay. Unit/route checks include real RSA person/service Access assertions and playback continuing during optional telemetry exceptions.

Reticle used a disposable PostgreSQL database through the actual local RPC adapter and app routes. Positive verdicts cover the weighted Winners response and order, intentional transport failure and retry, removal of a newly private title from historical public winners, and the signed-out moderator guard. The saved Winners flow has a declared network-body consequence and `asserted` grade. It requires its local fixture; random fixture identifiers/timestamps do not constitute a reusable production acceptance flow. Initial checks with incorrect text/array matchers failed and are not counted as passes. The actual signed-in moderator form and Stream telemetry remain unverified in a deployed environment. Temporary Reticle SDK/CSP wiring was removed before the final production build.
